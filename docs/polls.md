# Audience Polls

**Source of truth for both polls.** `run-of-show.md` and `slide-plan.md` point here — change the wording in this file only.

Productside webinars run two polls: one after the agenda to set up the topic, one at the close asking what the audience will do differently. Both use the `w-poll` layout (one question, options `01`–`05`).

**They share a single taxonomy: the seven capabilities, grouped into five options.** Poll 1 names five pains before the agenda names seven answers. Poll 2 asks the same five back as commitments. The delta between them is the signal. Share and Collaborate pair naturally (both are about working together), and Augment and Reuse pair naturally (both are about leveraging what the team already knows).

| | Poll 1 | Poll 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Slide | 8, after the agenda | after the close hero |
| Runs at | 0:08–0:10 | 0:52–0:54 |
| Open for | ~60 seconds | ~60 seconds |
| Read by | Kenny | Kenny |

---

## Poll 1 · Setup

**Question:**

> Which one hurts most right now?

**Options:**

| | Option | Capabilities |
|---|---|---|
| `01` | Our product thinking is scattered across six tools | Share + Collaborate |
| `02` | Changes happen and nobody reviews them first | Review |
| `03` | We cannot trace why a decision was made three months ago | Trace |
| `04` | Trying a new direction means risking the current one | Experiment |
| `05` | Every person on the team re-explains the product to their own AI | Augment + Reuse |

**How to run it.** Launch it right after the agenda, leave it open about a minute, then read the results out loud and **name the capability that covers the winner**. That one sentence turns the agenda from a feature list into a list of answers.

**The live steer:** if one option runs away with it, spend an extra minute on that capability and take the minute from one that landed low. Decide it in the moment.

## Poll 2 · Commitment

**Question:**

> Starting today, what will you do differently?

Productside's house closing question. Do not reword it.

**Options — each one is Poll 1's pain, ended, in the same order:**

| | Option | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| `01` | Put our product thinking in one shared workspace | `01` |
| `02` | Make changes reviewable before they become truth | `02` |
| `03` | Require a written reason with every change | `03` |
| `04` | Use branches to explore without breaking what works | `04` |
| `05` | Give our AI the same persistent context as the team | `05` |

**How to run it.** Launch it after the closing hero slide, before Q&A. **Compare the results against Poll 1 out loud.** Saying the comparison in the room is what makes the audience feel the arc close, and it costs fifteen seconds.

**Why the options mirror.** Each Poll 2 option is the Poll 1 pain in its resolved state, in the same order. That does three things at once: it recaps the session without a recap slide, it lets someone who picked `03` at the start see exactly what changed for them, and it makes the two result sets directly comparable rather than merely related.

**Why it earns its slot.** It converts watching into a commitment, and the Poll 1 to Poll 2 delta tells you which capability actually moved someone. That comparison is the most useful thing this session produces for Productside besides the registrations.

## Voice notes

Both polls follow the Copy Guidelines in the design system: second person, pain-led, conversational, no em dashes, no banned words.

**What they deliberately avoid:** repo, versioned, guardrails, evidence chain, front door *for requests*, intake, triage, and every other phrase that describes the mechanism instead of the result. The audience is product managers, not engineers. An option that names a thing you set up is an output. An option that names a thing that stops happening is an outcome, and outcomes are what Productside teaches.

**No option blames the team.** A standard that depends on someone remembering it is a badly built standard, so the pain is stated as a system failure, not as colleagues failing to comply. The abstract already promises guardrails that hold without making you the process cop; the poll should not describe the problem in the terms the session exists to eliminate.

**No option names a mechanism.** Repo, versioned, guardrails, evidence chain — all describe how it works rather than what it costs. The audience is product managers.

## For whoever loads these into the platform

- Two polls, one question each, five options each. Single choice, not multi-select — the whole point is forcing a priority.
- No "other" option and no free text. Five categories is the taxonomy the session teaches (seven capabilities grouped into five).
- Results need to be visible to the presenters in the moment, not just exported afterward.
- **Export both result sets after the session.** The comparison is the deliverable.

## Why these five, in this register

Both polls describe **outcomes**, because outcomes are what product managers actually care about. Not features in the abstract, not repository primitives, not technical mechanisms. Context scattered across tools, changes nobody reviewed, decisions nobody can trace back.

**Nothing in either poll has a villain.** "Changes happen and nobody reviews them first" has no one at fault in it. The system lets it happen. The capability fixes the system.

**Nothing names a mechanism.** No repo, no versioning, no guardrails, no front door. Those describe how it works. The poll asks what it costs.

**Poll 2 mirrors Poll 1 line for line.** Same order, same subjects, pain then resolution. Someone who picked `03` at the start can find `03` at the end and see precisely what changed, and the two result sets compare directly rather than merely relating.
