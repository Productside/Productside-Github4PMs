# Audience Polls

**Source of truth for both polls.** `run-of-show.md` and `slide-plan.md` point here — change the wording in this file only.

Productside webinars run two polls: one after the agenda to set up the topic, one at the close asking what the audience will do differently. Both use the `w-poll` layout (one question, options `01`–`05`).

**They share a single taxonomy: the five plays.** Poll 1 names five problems before the agenda names five answers. Poll 2 asks the same five back as commitments. The delta between them is the signal.

| | Poll 1 | Poll 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Slide | 8, after the agenda | after the close hero |
| Runs at | 0:08–0:10 | 0:52–0:54 |
| Open for | ~60 seconds | ~60 seconds |
| Read by | Kenny | Kenny |

---

pol## Poll 1 · Setup

**Question:**

> Which one hurts most right now?

**Options:**

| | Option | Play |
|---|---|---|
| `01` | We keep copying context everywhere | Context |
| `02` | Our feedback is scattered across email, chat, and comments | Communications |
| `03` | Our research and our decisions live in different places | Confidence |
| `04` | We don't do document standards well | Constitution |
| `05` | We don't have file locations to experiment | Community |

*`01` is swappable for "We have v.nn versions of files everywhere" — same play, more vivid, less tied to the AI thesis.*

**How to run it.** Launch it right after the agenda, leave it open about a minute, then read the results out loud and **name the play that covers the winner**. That one sentence turns the agenda from a feature list into a list of answers.

**The live steer:** if one option runs away with it, spend an extra minute on that play and take the minute from one that landed low. Decide it in the moment. The plays are written to survive that, except Play 5, which is the ending and does not get shaved.

---

## Poll 2 · Commitment

**Question:**

> Starting today, what will you do differently?

Productside's house closing question. Do not reword it.

**Options — each one is Poll 1's pain, ended, in the same order:**

| | Option | Answers |
|---|---|---|
| `01` | Keep context in one place instead of copying it | `01` |
| `02` | Keep feedback attached to the thing it's about | `02` |
| `03` | Keep research and decisions in the same place | `03` |
| `04` | Let the standards come with the template | `04` |
| `05` | Give half-formed ideas somewhere to live | `05` |

**How to run it.** Launch it after the closing hero slide, before Q&A. **Compare the results against Poll 1 out loud.** Saying the comparison in the room is what makes the audience feel the arc close, and it costs fifteen seconds.

**Why the options mirror.** Each Poll 2 option is the Poll 1 pain in its resolved state, in the same order. That does three things at once: it recaps the session without a recap slide, it lets someone who picked `03` at the start see exactly what changed for them, and it makes the two result sets directly comparable rather than merely related.

**Why it earns its slot.** It converts watching into a commitment, and the Poll 1 to Poll 2 delta tells you which play actually moved someone. That comparison is the most useful thing this session produces for Productside besides the registrations.

## Voice notes

Both polls follow the Copy Guidelines in the design system: second person, pain-led, conversational, no em dashes, no banned words.

**What they deliberately avoid:** repo, versioned, guardrails, evidence chain, front door *for requests*, intake, triage, and every other phrase that describes the mechanism instead of the result. The audience is product managers, not engineers. An option that names a thing you set up is an output. An option that names a thing that stops happening is an outcome, and outcomes are what Productside teaches.

**No option blames the team.** A standard that depends on someone remembering it is a badly built standard, so the pain is stated as a system failure, not as colleagues failing to comply. The abstract already promises guardrails that hold without making you the process cop; the poll should not describe the problem in the terms the play exists to eliminate.

**No option names a mechanism.** Repo, versioned, guardrails, evidence chain — all describe how it works rather than what it costs. The audience is product managers.

## For whoever loads these into the platform

- Two polls, one question each, five options each. Single choice, not multi-select — the whole point is forcing a priority.
- No "other" option and no free text. Five categories is the taxonomy the session teaches.
- Results need to be visible to the presenters in the moment, not just exported afterward.
- **Export both result sets after the session.** The comparison is the deliverable.

---

## Why these five, in this register

Both polls describe **documents**, because documents are what product managers actually handle all day. Not decisions in the abstract, not evidence chains, not repositories. Files that got copied, feedback that landed in three inboxes, research nobody can find next to the decision it informed.

**Nothing in either poll has a villain.** "We don't do document standards well" has no one at fault in it. An earlier draft read "we keep policing standards nobody follows," which made the product manager an enforcer and the team the problem. A standard that depends on someone remembering it is a badly built standard, and the play teaches exactly that.

**Nothing names a mechanism.** No repo, no versioning, no guardrails, no front door. Those describe how it works. The poll asks what it costs.

**Poll 2 mirrors Poll 1 line for line.** Same order, same subjects, pain then resolution. Someone who picked `03` at the start can find `03` at the end and see precisely what changed, and the two result sets compare directly rather than merely relating.
