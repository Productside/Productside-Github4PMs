## The Summary Frame

This is not a recap. It is a reframe. The question you started with was whether your team has a place where its thinking can compound. The answer is not "replace everything" — it is "add the missing layer."

---

### KEEP What Your Tools Do Best

Your existing tools are not the problem. They are good at what they were built for.

- **Docs for storage.** Google Docs, Confluence, Notion, SharePoint — they hold documents, they allow co-editing, they support rich formatting. Nothing in this session replaces them for what they do well.

- **Jira for tracking.** Sprints, epics, story points, velocity charts. If your engineering team runs on Jira, it keeps running on Jira. The work tracker tracks work.

- **Slack for conversation.** Real-time coordination, quick questions, status updates, the social fabric of a distributed team. Slack is where the day happens.

None of these go away. They each do a job, and they do it well enough that replacing them creates more disruption than value.

---

### ADD GitHub For

What none of those tools do — what the gap has been all along — is give you version, context, review, and AI access in the same place.

- **Version + Context + Review + AI.** A single layer where the product's thinking is versioned (every change is tracked), contextualized (the reasoning travels with the decision), reviewable (someone can look at a proposed change and comment before it merges), and AI-accessible (the assistant reads the repository, not your morning re-explanation).

- **The layer where change needs memory.** Not every document needs this. Your meeting notes probably do not. Your Slack standup definitely does not. But your product strategy, your decision records, your research findings, your constraints and commitments — those need memory. They need to be findable six months from now, with the discussion that shaped them still attached.

GitHub is not a replacement for your tool stack. It is the connective tissue that your tool stack is missing.

---

### RESULT

When the missing layer is in place, four things shift:

**Your team works from shared context, not scattered artifacts.** The product's current state lives in one place that everyone — including the AI — can read. No more "let me find the doc" or "I think that decision was in a Slack thread somewhere." The single source of truth is not a phrase on a slide. It is a repository someone can open.

**Decisions are faster because the reasoning travels with the change.** When a pull request carries the "why" alongside the "what," the reviewer does not need a meeting to understand the context. The approval happens asynchronously, in writing, with the reasoning visible to anyone who reads the history later. Decisions that used to take a meeting now take a comment.

**AI is smarter because it reads the repo, not your morning re-explanation.** Every AI tool you use today starts from zero. You explain the product, the constraints, the recent decisions, the audience. With a repository, you stop explaining and start directing. The AI reads what the team already knows. The conversation begins at the frontier, not at the foundation.

**Handoffs are seamless because the record exists.** When someone joins the team, leaves the team, or picks up a project after a gap, the repository is the onboarding. Not a deck someone has to schedule time to walk through. Not a series of "let me introduce you to the people who know." The history is there. The decisions are there. The reasoning is there. The new person reads it and starts contributing.

---

> **Use GitHub where change needs memory. Keep doing what other tools do best.**
