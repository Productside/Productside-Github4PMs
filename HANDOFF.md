## Next: PM-problem rewrite of play page copy

**The single biggest content task remaining.** The play pages and their slide counterparts currently explain what GitHub features do. They need to lead with the PM problems those features solve. The audience is product managers who have never typed `git commit`. They will not care about Discussions vs Issues until they hear their own pain described first.

The reframe pattern, per Dean's voice: **name the PM's pain, concede what they do now, show how the tool changes the outcome.**

Example shift for Play 2 (Communications):
- **Now:** "Discussion: should we do something about this. Issue: we have decided to investigate or change."
- **Reframed:** "Your team has an idea. Right now it lives in Slack, dies in a thread, and six months later someone proposes the same thing again. The fork: is this still a question, or have we already decided? GitHub gives that fork a home."

Example shift for Play 4 (Constitution):
- **Now:** "Three files, three jobs. CONSTITUTION.md overrides CLAUDE.md overrides README."
- **Reframed:** "Every PM has rules that should never be broken: no client names in public, no shipping without legal review. Right now those rules live in someone's head. The three layers make them findable, enforceable, and impossible to override by accident."

This applies to both the markdown play pages (`docs/slides/00-10`) AND the corresponding PowerPoint slides. The diagrams are fine; the surrounding copy needs the reframe. Pages 00 (the problem) and 10 (the honest part) are already problem-first and probably need the least work.

## After the webinar: publishing this Project

**Recommended, but it cannot simply be flipped.** Roughly two thirds of what is here should never be public, and one part carries third-party restrictions.

### Must not go public

- **`research/`** — the digest files quote and summarize two sources with explicit redistribution restrictions. The Aakash Gupta / Shubham Saboo piece is paywalled, and `pm-github-starter-kit` states in its own README that it "should not be redistributed." See the top of `research/findings/third-party-gems.md`. Publishing our digests of them is a third-party rights problem, not just an etiquette one.
- **`docs/*.docx`** — Dean's internal training proposal and the marketing copy template. Internal working documents.
- **`docs/mi-repo-publication.md`** — names an org secret, records commit-authorship housekeeping, and documents another Project's configuration gaps.
- **`docs/kenny-prep.md`** — this is the important one. It characterizes a named colleague's readiness, names what he must drill, and plans for him getting stuck on camera. It was written to help him and it would not read that way in public. **Do not publish it, and do not publish a lightly-edited version of it.**

### Could go public, and is genuinely worth publishing

- **`docs/diagrams/`** — 29 diagrams of PM-relevant GitHub workflows, all originally drawn. This is the real asset and the strongest lead-generation artifact in the Project.
- **`README.md`**, **`CLAUDE.md`**, **`CONSTITUTION.md`** — the three-layer pattern, demonstrated rather than described.
- **`scripts/split-diagrams.py`** — small, useful, and it shows the diagrams are generated rather than hand-maintained.
- A **run-of-show with the failure-recovery and per-person notes removed**, if there is appetite. It is a genuinely useful artifact for anyone running a live technical session. It is also a rewrite, not a redaction.

### How to do it

**Do not flip this Project. Publish a new one.** Trying to sanitize this repository in place means the history still contains the research digests and the prep notes, and git history is not the release archive.

Create `Productside-GitHub-Plays` (or similar), copy in only the publishable material, give it a fresh history, and follow the same checklist used for the market intelligence Project: `LICENSE` with the 280 Group copyright line and plain-language "as is" preamble, the canonical positioning statement in the README, `TRADEMARKS.md`, links to the live Privacy and Terms pages, and the content guard with `BLOCKED_TERMS` granted before the flip.

**Timing:** after the webinar, not before. There is no reason to add a publication review to a week that already has a live broadcast in it.

## Conventions worth preserving

Recorded in [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md), repeated here because they are easy to lose:

- **No git tutorial.** One deliberate exception: Dean's three-minute origin story at 0:04. It does not license adding command-line content anywhere else.
- **Every concept carries its own limit and adoption test.** A limitation admitted next to the thing it limits reads as expertise; batched at the end it reads as covering yourself.
- **`docs/diagrams.md` is the source of truth.** `docs/diagrams/` is generated. Edit the former, run the script.
- **Productside is a services firm.** The webinar teaches GitHub, so repo, issue, and pull request are the subject matter and get used plainly. The rule bites on how Productside's own materials are described.
